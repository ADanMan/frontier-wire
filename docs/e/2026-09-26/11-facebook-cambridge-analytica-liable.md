## English version

A jury has found Facebook liable for deceiving users in the long-running Cambridge Analytica litigation, [CBS News reports](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/facebook-liable-deceiving-users-cambridge-analytica/). The 2018 scandal, in which tens of millions of users' data ended up with a political consulting firm, dragged through regulators, congressional hearings and courts for nearly a decade — and has now produced a ruling on the actual merits.

A liability finding by a jury is unusual in big-tech cases of this scale: companies typically prefer multimillion-dollar settlements precisely to avoid an admission-adjacent outcome like this one. A verdict means something different from a settlement — it establishes that the company did, in fact, mislead users, rather than simply agreeing to pay to make the case go away.

The story is also getting steady attention on Hacker News, from an audience well used to seeing cases like this get buried in procedural delays and fizzle out without a clear result.

### Why it matters

A liability verdict, rather than a settlement, sets a stronger legal precedent for future suits over platforms' handling of user data — and shows that even decade-long cases against big tech sometimes reach a substantive ruling.
