## English version

Early this morning, burglars [broke into](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/thieves-broke-into-a-french-museum-and-made-off-with-renoir-paintings-worth-more-than-25-million-180989462/) the Renoir Museum on the French Riviera and made off with four paintings worth more than $2.5 million combined. According to Smithsonian Magazine, the thieves dropped two of the four canvases while fleeing the scene — meaning they ultimately got away with only half their haul.

The magazine hasn't yet detailed which specific works were stolen, what condition the abandoned paintings are in, or whether there are any suspects; the investigation appears to be in its early stages.

The fact that the thieves apparently dropped half their loot in a hurry says less about criminal sophistication than it does about the practical difficulty of the job itself: carrying four paintings out, it turns out, was harder than planning the break-in.

### Why it matters

Every theft like this carries the risk that a one-of-a-kind painting disappears into a closed private collection for good; the getaway going wrong enough to leave two canvases behind is a rare case of the thieves' own haste working in the museum's favor.
