The Cygnus XL spacecraft is expected to carry approximately 11,000 pounds of cargo. A Falcon 9 rocket would launch it from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida. The advisory illustrates the story with a photograph from the earlier CRS-24 mission, which launched in April. That historical image is not evidence of a CRS-25 launch. After arrival, the schedule calls for capture with the Canadarm2 robotic arm on December 1. NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney are assigned that operation in the announcement. Ground controllers would then install the spacecraft at the Unity module's Earth-facing port for unloading. Launch, arrival, capture and installation are distinct steps rather than interchangeable descriptions of one event. NASA lists research intended to improve drug delivery, predict cancer spread, advance treatments for muscle loss, improve radiation sensing and support food production for long-duration missions. It would then depart carrying disposal items and other unneeded cargo, which would be destroyed with the vehicle during atmospheric re-entry.

A prelaunch teleconference is scheduled for November 23. Launch coverage is planned to begin at 10:25 a.m. EST on November 29. Arrival coverage is scheduled for 1:10 a.m. EST on December 1, followed by capture at 2:35 a.m. EST.
