## English version

Saudi Arabia has shut down its East-West oil pipeline following a drone attack, [Al Jazeera reports](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/9/12/saudi-arabia-east-west-oil-pipeline-drone-attack-iraq?traffic_source=rss). Riyadh says the attack originated in Iraq. Satellite imagery confirmed damage to the pipeline.

The East-West pipeline isn't just any pipe — it's one of the main routes carrying Saudi oil to the Red Sea coast, built specifically as an alternative to the Strait of Hormuz, the single most vulnerable chokepoint in global oil supply. Striking this particular route isn't a random target choice — it's an attack on infrastructure that matters precisely because it was meant to reduce dependence on Hormuz.

The release of satellite imagery is a detail that moves the story from "competing claims" into confirmed fact: the damage is visible regardless of whose account of the attack you believe. What remains contested is the source of the attack — Riyadh names Iraq, but multiple armed groups operate from Iraqi territory, and a single statement isn't enough to definitively attribute the strike to one actor.

For oil markets, any shutdown of a major Saudi export route is a signal traders track regardless of how much oil buyers actually end up losing physically — the demonstration of vulnerability in an alternative route carries its own price.

### Why it matters

An attack on a pipeline built specifically to bypass the Strait of Hormuz shows the region has no truly attack-proof oil export routes left — even the ones designed as backups are vulnerable.
