More than six years ago, Chess.com joked that the pandemic-disrupted 2020 FIDE Candidates Tournament would get "finished" in Antarctica. Now, [as the site itself reports](https://www.chess.com/news/view/first-fide-rated-chess-tournament-antarctica-south-pole), rated chess really is heading to the world's coldest continent — the first-ever FIDE-rated tournament to be held in Antarctica.

Chess.com is explicit about it: "No, it's not an April Fool's" — a deliberate nod to the old joke, meant to separate it from the real news this time. For the chess world, it's a symbolic milestone: rated tournaments have been staged in plenty of odd places before, but the South Pole had stayed purely hypothetical, a punchline rather than an actual entry on FIDE's calendar.

The event itself probably won't shake up the top of the rankings, but it's a neat example of a six-year-old joke about an impossible venue quietly turning into an actual logistics project — someone eventually organized a real tournament in the place players used to get sent to only as a meme.
