## Русская версия

Университет Queensland [сообщил о выводе платформы NASTIC в стартап Accretion Bio](https://news.uq.edu.au/2026-10-uq-technology-opens-new-front-alzheimers-and-parkinsons-drug-discovery). Сообщение опубликовано 19 октября. Технология позволяет исследовать движение и объединение белков внутри живых нейронов и наблюдать, как на это действуют потенциальные лекарственные соединения. Первое заявленное направление компании — нейродегенеративные заболевания. Новость касается инструмента для раннего поиска препаратов; нового одобренного лечения в ней не представлено.

NASTIC расшифровывается как Nanoscale Spatiotemporal Indexing Clustering. В центре подхода одновременно находятся пространство и время: важно не только увидеть белок, но и проследить, где он движется и как формирует группы. По объяснению исследователей, такие наблюдения позволяют изучать поведение молекул внутри живой клетки. Это более определённая задача, чем общее обещание «увидеть болезнь», и именно её следует проверять при обсуждении применения.

Университет связывает разработку с многолетней работой Queensland Brain Institute под руководством Frederic Meunier. Коммерциализацию поддержала UniQuest, а стартап получил поддержку венчурной компании Antler. Его основатель и руководитель — Phillip Craig; технологические сооснователи — Meunier и Tristan Wallis. Accretion Bio намерена применять инструмент в программах фармацевтических и биотехнологических партнёров и развивать собственное направление поиска препаратов. ## English version

The University of Queensland has [announced the commercialisation of NASTIC through Accretion Bio](https://news.uq.edu.au/2026-10-uq-technology-opens-new-front-alzheimers-and-parkinsons-drug-discovery). Its October 19 release describes a platform for investigating protein movement and clustering inside living neurons, including changes associated with potential drug compounds. The company's initial focus is neurodegeneration. This is an announcement about early drug-discovery infrastructure, rather than the introduction of an approved treatment.

NASTIC stands for Nanoscale Spatiotemporal Indexing Clustering. Space and time both matter to the proposed use: researchers want to follow where proteins move and how they gather, rather than merely detect their presence. The university describes studying molecular behaviour within a living cell. That is a more specific and testable purpose than a broad promise to see a disease unfolding.

The technology grew from research at the Queensland Brain Institute led by Frederic Meunier. UniQuest supported its commercialisation, and venture firm Antler is backing the startup. Founder and chief executive Phillip Craig works with technology co-founders Meunier and Tristan Wallis. A dedicated company represents an organisational transition towards partner-facing research. Its creation is not, by itself, a measurement of the platform's effectiveness.

Accretion Bio intends to apply NASTIC to pharmaceutical and biotechnology partners' programmes while developing its own drug-discovery programme. The university also says industry-funded research using the platform is already under way at the institute. However, the announcement does not report clinical trial outcomes, identify a successful medicine or quantify how often this approach produces a useful drug compared with existing methods.

That distinction changes how the news should be read. If a compound alters protein movement or clustering, researchers obtain an observation about a cellular process. Establishing a therapeutic benefit requires other evidence. This is a boundary on the inference, not a dismissal of the measurement: better information about a mechanism may help select the next experiment, but cannot stand in for its outcome.

The university identifies receptor clustering in immunology and oncology as possible applications beyond neurodegeneration. Those are proposed directions, rather than a catalogue of demonstrated clinical achievements. The company is scheduled to participate at AusBiotech on October 21–23, which remains in the future at publication. Nor does the company announcement mean the underlying scientific method was first published today.

### Why it matters

Many tests separate a molecular hypothesis from a medicine. NASTIC is being positioned near the beginning of that process, where researchers examine protein behaviour inside living cells. The commercial transition should be judged through the quality of the resulting evidence and subsequent validation, while keeping a research tool distinct from a therapeutic outcome.
