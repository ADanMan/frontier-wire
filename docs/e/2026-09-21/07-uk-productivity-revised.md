## English version

UK labor productivity in the decade after the 2008 financial crisis grew at [almost twice the pace previously estimated](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/09/uk-productivity-higher-than-we-had-thought.html), according to new official figures cited by the Marginal Revolution blog. The measure in question is output per hour worked — the standard yardstick of labor productivity — and the revision makes Britain look far less of an outlier among developed economies than it's been portrayed as for the past decade and a half.

This matters beyond being a statistical footnote. The "UK productivity puzzle" — the country's persistent lag behind other G7 economies since 2008 — has been a central topic in debates over Britain's weak wage growth and stagnant living standards. If the baseline numbers underlying that puzzle were understated, then some of the explanations economists have offered over the years need revisiting too — not because they were wrong in principle, but because they were solving a slightly different problem than the one that actually existed.

Retroactive statistical revisions are routine, but the scale here is telling: a near-doubling isn't a technical tweak, it's an admission that the UK economy looked considerably more stagnant than it actually was.

### Why it matters

If UK productivity grew at nearly twice the previously estimated pace after 2008, then the famous "UK productivity puzzle" was partly an artifact of understated statistics — not purely a real structural lag in the economy.
