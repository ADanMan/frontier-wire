По марокканской конституции король формально назначает премьера, но обязан выбирать кандидата от партии-победителя парламентских выборов — то есть само по себе назначение не сенсация, а вот то, что победившая партия выдвинула женщину, а король это утвердил, для региона куда заметнее. Мансури предстоит теперь формировать правительство — задача, где реальная политика начинается уже после красивой новости о «первой».

## English version

Morocco's King Mohammed VI has appointed Fatima Zahra Mansouri as the country's prime minister — the first woman to hold the post in its history. The move follows [her centre-right party's win in last week's parliamentary elections](https://www.dw.com/en/morocco-king-appoints-first-woman-prime-minister/a-79479722); before this, Mansouri served as mayor of Marrakesh, one of the country's best-known cities.

Under Morocco's constitution the king formally appoints the prime minister, but is bound to pick from the winning party — so the appointment itself isn't the surprise. What stands out is that the winning party ran a woman at the top and the king confirmed her. Mansouri now has to actually build a government, which is where the real test starts, well past the "first woman" headline.

Worth keeping in mind: Moroccan public life has been visibly shifting toward more women in politics for years, but heading a government is still rare for a woman anywhere in the Arab world. What Mansouri does with the mandate in her first months will matter far more than the appointment itself.

### Why it matters

A woman leading the government of an Arab monarchy is a rare symbol — but a symbol is not a guarantee of policy change. What happens inside the cabinet next is the real story.
