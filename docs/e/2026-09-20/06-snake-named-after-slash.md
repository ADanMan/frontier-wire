## English version

Scientists [have described a new snake species from New Guinea and named it after Guns N' Roses guitarist Slash](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260918024816.htm) — Lielaphis slashi, ScienceDaily reports. Slash has long and openly loved reptiles, and this isn't the first species named after him, but this one comes backed by rigorous scientific procedure: genetic testing and physical differences confirmed the reddish-brown groundsnake is indeed a distinct species that had never been formally described before.

The story isn't only about a fun name. Naming a species after a well-known person is a common practice in taxonomy — it draws attention to the discovery and, sometimes, helps fund further research into rare and understudied ecosystems like the New Guinea rainforests, where species still turn up that have never before appeared in scientific literature.

The fact that discoveries like this still happen in the 21st century is itself notable: New Guinea remains one of the few places on the planet where biodiversity is understood so incompletely that new vertebrate species are found regularly, not once a decade.

### Why it matters

Formally describing a new snake species isn't just a quirky-name curiosity — it's a reminder that even today, science knows only a fraction of the species living in New Guinea's rainforests.
