## Русская версия

[NASA 6 октября рассказала о Lunar Grounding Challenge](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/prizes-challenges-crowdsourcing-program/center-of-excellence-for-collaborative-innovation-coeci/lunar-grounding-challenge/) — конкурсе концепций защиты астронавта от электростатического разряда при возвращении к аппарату. Ходьба и окружающая плазма могут заряжать скафандр; в тени условия особенно сложны. Контакт с модулем другого потенциала создаёт риск искры. Конкурс открылся 5 октября, приём решений указан до 15 января 2027 года, общий призовой фонд — до 150 тысяч долларов. Речь о поиске решения для будущих операций, а не о сообщении об уже произошедшей аварии.

[Техническая страница конкурса](https://work.crowdplat.com/challenge/lunar-grounding-challenge) добавляет конкретный масштаб задачи. В расчётном сценарии отрицательно заряженный астронавт имеет потенциал минус 100 киловольт или более по модулю, посадочный аппарат — около плюс 100 вольт. Предельная масса решения должна быть менее 20 килограммов, худшее время разряда — 30 секунд. На первом этапе физический прототип не требуется; до пяти финалистов смогут перейти к уточнению концепций. Это условия конкурсной постановки, а не измерения на действующей лунной экспедиции или подтверждённые характеристики готового устройства.

## English version

[NASA described the Lunar Grounding Challenge on October 6](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/prizes-challenges-crowdsourcing-program/center-of-excellence-for-collaborative-innovation-coeci/lunar-grounding-challenge/), seeking concepts to protect astronauts from electrostatic discharge when returning to a lander. Walking and ambient plasma can charge a spacesuit, with shadows creating particularly difficult conditions. Contact with a lander at a different potential can produce a spark. The challenge opened October 5, lists a January 15, 2027 submission deadline and offers up to $150,000 in total prizes. This concerns a problem for future operations, rather than an announcement of an accident that has already occurred.

The [challenge's technical page](https://work.crowdplat.com/challenge/lunar-grounding-challenge) adds a concrete scale to the scenario. Its design case considers a negatively charged astronaut at minus 100 kilovolts or greater magnitude and a lander around plus 100 volts. The proposed solution must weigh less than 20 kilograms; the worst-case discharge duration is 30 seconds. Phase one requires no physical prototype, and up to five finalists may advance to concept refinement. These are conditions of the challenge, rather than measurements from an operating lunar expedition or established specifications of a finished device.

Our reading is that the familiar word grounding can conceal the central question: which potentials are being compared, and how will they be equalized? The future procedure requires controlled interaction between a suited person and a vehicle. The announcement defines an intended outcome and environment without selecting a universal implementation. Readers therefore cannot infer that adding an ordinary terrestrial wire, or copying a household static-control trick, would meet the requirement. We propose no safety-system design here. Assessing one would require separate engineering work and verification under the relevant conditions.

For readers following lunar technology, the noteworthy feature is this level of specificity. The focus falls on a transition between operations: an astronaut finishes an excursion and interacts with the vehicle again. In our interpretation, that highlights how a subsystem must fit a sequence of actions. Useful questions for eventual challenge results include how constraints are addressed, what calculations establish and what still needs testing. An open competition is not a selected design; a selected concept is not installed equipment. Those stages carry different levels of evidence and should be described accordingly.

### Why it matters

The announcement exposes one concrete unresolved engineering question alongside large lunar ambitions. Defined requirements make it discussable without promising a finished outcome. Our conclusion is to follow the movement from concept to verification while keeping design assumptions distinct from a tested system. That transition will establish whether a proposed approach can function as part of an actual return-to-lander procedure, rather than merely appear convincing in a competition submission.
