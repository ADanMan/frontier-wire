Netanyahu's speech itself unfolded against a backdrop of walkouts inside the chamber, with several delegations leaving the hall during his address. Rights groups cited by Al Jazeera in connection with the event accuse the UN of giving a platform to a man they call a "war criminal" — a reference to the International Criminal Court's arrest warrant against Netanyahu.

The timing of the two events — street arrests of celebrities and the visible walkout of delegations inside — shows that opposition to Netanyahu's policies is registering at two levels at once: institutionally, inside the UN itself, and on the street, among ordinary New Yorkers and public figures willing to risk arrest to make a statement.

Celebrity involvement in protests like this is nothing new for New York during General Assembly week, when dozens of world leaders converge on the city along with heavy security and, predictably, large numbers of protesters. But the arrest of a recognizable figure like Sarandon tends to draw far more attention to an action than an equivalent protest without well-known participants.

Mass arrests during Netanyahu's UN address underline how polarized reaction to Israeli policy remains even in New York — the city that hosts the General Assembly every year. For summit organizers, it's also a practical challenge: keeping the event secure without turning the city into a zone of permanent protest.
